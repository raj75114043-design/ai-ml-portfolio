# SOP-QA-001 — Investigation of Out-of-Specification (OOS) Results

> Synthetic SOP for demonstration purposes only. Timelines are illustrative.

## 1. Purpose
This procedure defines how Out-of-Specification (OOS) test results are investigated so that the root cause is identified and batch disposition decisions are scientifically justified.

## 2. Scope
This procedure applies to all OOS results obtained for raw materials, in-process samples, finished products and stability samples tested by the Quality Control laboratory.

## 3. Definition
An OOS result is any test result that falls outside the registered specification or acceptance criteria established in the product dossier, pharmacopoeia or internal specifications.

## 4. Phase Ia Investigation
The analyst informs the QC supervisor immediately and no later than the same working day. The analyst and supervisor check for obvious errors such as calculation mistakes, incorrect dilution, instrument malfunction or wrong standard. Phase Ia must be completed within one working day. If an obvious assignable laboratory error is confirmed, the original result is invalidated and the test is repeated.

## 5. Phase Ib Investigation
If no obvious error is found, a hypothesis-driven laboratory investigation is performed using a predefined checklist covering sample preparation, reagents, instrument qualification status, system suitability and analyst training. Re-measurement of the original preparation may be performed only to test a documented hypothesis. Phase Ib must be completed within five working days.

## 6. Phase II Investigation
If no laboratory root cause is identified, the investigation is escalated to a full-scale Phase II investigation involving Production, Quality Assurance and Engineering. Phase II reviews batch manufacturing records, equipment logs, environmental data and raw material history. Phase II must be completed within thirty calendar days, with a documented extension approved by the Head of Quality if required.

## 7. Retesting and Resampling
Retesting is performed by a second qualified analyst on the original sample, with the number of retests defined in advance in the investigation plan. Averaging of results must not be used to mask an OOS result. Resampling is permitted only when the original sample is shown to be non-representative or compromised.

## 8. Batch Disposition and CAPA
A batch with a confirmed OOS result must be rejected unless a scientifically justified invalidation is documented and approved by Quality Assurance. Corrective and preventive actions are raised through the CAPA system in accordance with SOP-QA-004.
